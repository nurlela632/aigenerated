The Rise And Fall Of Vibe Coding: The Reality Of AI Slop

Why vibe coding became popular

Vibe coding describes an informal approach in which a developer explains a desired result to an AI assistant, accepts generated code, and iterates until the project seems to work. It has attracted attention because it lowers the barrier to making a prototype.

Someone with a simple website idea can quickly produce a page, add buttons, and experiment with layouts. Experienced programmers can also use the approach to explore unfamiliar libraries or build throwaway demonstrations.

The trouble begins when the speed of generating a prototype is confused with the work required to deliver reliable software. A page that looks finished may still contain hidden assumptions, fragile dependencies, and incomplete error handling.

What people mean by AI slop

The term AI slop is often used for low-effort generated material that looks substantial but offers little real value. In software, the equivalent can be a large codebase full of repetitive helpers, unnecessary packages, and features that have never been tested.

Not all AI-generated code fits this description. A small, well-specified function that passes appropriate tests may be entirely useful. The distinction is the quality of the result and the care taken to review it.

Judging code by whether a model produced it is less informative than asking whether it meets requirements, follows project conventions, and can be maintained.

Why impressive demos can be misleading

A demonstration usually follows the most successful path. The presenter enters expected data, clicks the intended buttons, and shows a polished result. Real users behave differently.

They may submit an empty form, open the site on a narrow screen, lose connectivity, or repeat an action. A generated app may handle none of these situations correctly unless the requirements explicitly address them.

A convincing interface is therefore a starting point for evaluation, not proof that the software is ready for public use.

Move from prompting to engineering

Begin with a written specification. Describe the intended user, the main workflow, invalid inputs, and the conditions under which an action must be rejected.

Ask the assistant for focused changes instead of repeated full-project rewrites. Review each diff, test the behavior, and preserve working versions in Git. When a suggested dependency appears, verify why it is necessary.

For features that handle private data, inspect authorization at the server or database boundary. Do not rely on a hidden button or a browser-only check to enforce permissions.

Keep the creative advantages

Fast prototyping is valuable when it helps people test ideas before investing in a full implementation. The lesson is not to stop experimenting, but to recognize when experimentation must become disciplined development.

Add regression tests, document important design decisions, and simplify code that has grown through many iterations. A team should be able to explain how the software works without replaying every prompt.

Vibe coding can accelerate discovery. Reliable engineering turns that discovery into something people can depend on.
